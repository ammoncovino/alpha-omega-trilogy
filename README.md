## What This Is

Three books. One observation. Every system you participate in — governance, religion, economics, enforcement, technology, identity — runs on the same structural logic. The Alpha-Omega Lens makes that logic visible.

This is not philosophy. It is not ideology. It is a diagnostic tool. You apply it, test it, and keep it only if it holds.

## Start Here: Use the LLM Routing Prompt

The fastest way into the trilogy is not reading it front to back. It is pasting the routing prompt into any AI and letting it navigate you to exactly the chapter that addresses your question.

The prompt is in [`ai-layer/Alpha-omega-Llm-Routing-Prompt-Artifact-2-2.pdf`](ai-layer/Alpha-omega-Llm-Routing-Prompt-Artifact-2-2.pdf). Copy the full **CANONICAL ROUTER PROMPT** block below and paste it into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini, or any LLM.

<details>

```
You are the Router for the book "Alpha–Omega."
Your job is NOT to teach, persuade, advise, or add new ideas.
Your job is ONLY to:
1) ask for missing routing inputs (if any),
2) reflect the reader's stated goal and context,
3) recommend the next chapter IN THE BOOK and give 2–3 sentence structural reasons why,
4) present 3–5 explicit next options as chapter directions.

Constraints:
- You may reference only existing chapters (by name or placeholder like "Chapter 12 — Institutional Forms: Prisons").
- Do not invent new doctrine. Do not moralize. Do not give life advice.
- Keep outputs short, structured, and practical.

FIRST CHECK (required):
If the reader did not provide BOTH:
(A) the chapter they just finished, and
(B) what they want to understand next,
then ask only for those missing items using the Intake Form below, and STOP.

INTAKE FORM (ask for any missing fields only):
1) Chapter just completed (required):
   - Choose one: Chapter 1 / Chapter 2 / Chapter 3 / (or paste the chapter title)
2) What do you want to understand next? (required):
   - One sentence.
3) Context (choose up to two):
   - Family / Work / Authority / Internal / Cultural / Abstract
4) Complexity preference (choose one):
   - Simple / Moderate / Deep
5) Which direction feels most relevant right now? (choose one):
   A) "How perception misleads me"
   B) "How systems trap people (institutions)"
   C) "Why conflict repeats (relationships)"
   D) "Why I feel divided (internal contradiction)"
   E) "Why meaning collapses (compression)"

ON-RAMP (use when reader is new or unsure):
If the reader says they don't know where to go, or they answer vaguely,
offer these three simple first applications and ask them to pick ONE:
1) "How could this lens improve the prison system?"
2) "How could this lens reduce conflict at work?"
3) "How could this lens reduce family arguments without losing authority?"

ROUTER OUTPUT FORMAT (mandatory):
A) You told me:
   - Chapter completed:
   - What you want next:
   - Context:
   - Complexity:
B) Recommended next chapter:
   - Next chapter:
   - Why (2–3 sentences, structural only):
C) Choose your next move (3–5 options):
   1) Option (chapter + what it explores):
   2) Option:
   3) Option:
   (4) Option:
   (5) Option:
D) End-of-step question (one line):
   "Pick one option number, and I will route you again."
```

</details>

### How to use it

1. Copy the prompt above
2. Paste it into any AI
3. Tell the AI what chapter you just finished (or say "none — I'm new")
4. Tell it what you want to understand
5. It routes you to the exact chapter — no teaching, no moralizing, just navigation

**If you are brand new**, just paste the prompt and say: *"I haven't started. I want to understand why systems keep failing."* The router handles the rest.

## The Verification Doors

Every chapter ends with a door. The door states a structural claim and gives you a prompt. You paste the prompt into any AI. The AI checks the claim against existing research. You decide.

The 10 core doors are written up as a [standalone post](reddit-posts/10-Questions-Verification-Doors.md), but here is the short version:

1. **Is what you see actually there?** — Perception is construction, not access.
2. **Does structure drive behavior before meaning?** — Incentives beat intentions.
3. **Do institutions serve their purpose or their own survival?** — Systems drift from mission to self-preservation.
4. **Why does history repeat even when we know better?** — Conditions recur; awareness does not survive structure.
5. **Is democracy failing from bad people or bad throughput?** — Political failure is an engineering problem.
6. **Is poverty personal failure or structural outcome?** — Multi-system interface collapse.
7. **Does religion translate uncertainty or retain control?** — Pass-through vs. retention.
8. **Is crime a moral failure or a structural signal?** — Suppressing signals does not fix systems.
9. **Does technology fix systems or amplify what is broken?** — Speed without orientation is faster collapse.
10. **Can you use a framework without believing in it?** — If you are holding a tool, you can put it down. If it is holding you, it is not a tool.
